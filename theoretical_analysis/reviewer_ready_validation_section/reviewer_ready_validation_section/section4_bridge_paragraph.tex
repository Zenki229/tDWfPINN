The derivative-level diagnostics in Section~\ref{subsec:num-valid} guide the parameter choices used below.  They show that Monte Carlo integration retains the expected $M^{-1/2}$ sampling rate but becomes more sensitive to the denominator cutoff as $\alpha\to2$, while Gauss--Jacobi quadrature achieves much faster convergence for smooth transformed kernels with moderate $M$.  They also show that unnecessarily large Gauss--Jacobi sizes may expose raw endpoint quotients to roundoff-dominated cancellation.  Accordingly, the PINN experiments use moderate Gauss--Jacobi quadrature sizes and the fixed Monte Carlo cutoff specified below, unless otherwise stated.
